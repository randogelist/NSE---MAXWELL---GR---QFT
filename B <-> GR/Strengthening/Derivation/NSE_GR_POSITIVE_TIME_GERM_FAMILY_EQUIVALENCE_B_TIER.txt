3. PERIODIC FOURIER AND PRESSURE LEMMAS
-------------------------------------

Write xi_k=2pi k for k in Z^3, and use inhomogeneous Sobolev norms

  ||v||_{H^m}^2 = sum_k (1+|xi_k|^2)^m |vhat(k)|^2.     (3.1)

The zero mode is included. These norms therefore retain arbitrary means.

3.1 Algebra and point evaluation.

For integer m>=2, sum_k(1+|xi_k|^2)^(-m) is finite. Cauchy-Schwarz gives

  sum_k |vhat(k)| <= C_m ||v||_{H^m}.

The weight inequality

  <xi_{j+l}>^m <= C_m(<xi_j>^m+<xi_l>^m)

and the convolution estimate ||a*b||_{ell^2}<=||a||_{ell^2}||b||_{ell^1}
give the product bound

  ||vw||_{H^m} <= C_m ||v||_{H^m}||w||_{H^m}.           (3.2)

The same bound holds for vector/tensor products using the finite-dimensional
Euclidean/Frobenius norms. The Fourier ell^1 bound also proves bounded point
evaluation, |v(0)|<=C_m||v||_{H^m}.

For m>r+3/2, the same Cauchy-Schwarz argument with |xi_k|^r gives

  ||v||_{C^r} <= C_{m,r}||v||_{H^m}.                   (3.3)

Conversely, on the compact torus, ||v||_{H^m}<=C_m||v||_{C^m} for integer m.
Consequently convergence in every H^m is equivalent to convergence in the
spatial C-infinity topology.

3.2 Leray and pressure multipliers.

Set

  P(k)=I-k tensor k/|k|^2, k!=0; P(0)=I.

Then P is bounded on every H^m with norm <=1, commutes with spatial
derivatives, and P div maps tensor H^{m+1} continuously to vector H^m.

For a tensor field A, define a scalar R(A) by

  R(A)hat(0)=0,
  R(A)hat(k)=-sum_{i,j} xi_{k,i}xi_{k,j}/|xi_k|^2
                              * Ahat_ij(k), k!=0.       (3.4)

The matrix (xi_i xi_j/|xi|^2) has Frobenius norm one. Therefore
||R(A)||_{H^m}<=||A||_{H^m}. Define the normalized bilinear pressure operator

  Q(v,w)=R(v tensor w)-R(v tensor w)(0),
  Pi(v)=Q(v,v).                                        (3.5)

For m>=2, Q:H^m x H^m -> H^m is continuous bilinear, and Pi is a continuous
quadratic map. In particular,

  ||Pi(v)||_{H^m} <= C_m||v||_{H^m}^2,
  ||Pi(v)-Pi(w)||_{H^m}
    <= C_m(||v||_{H^m}+||w||_{H^m})||v-w||_{H^m}.       (3.6)

3.3 Pressure is determined by velocity up to gauge.

Let (v,p) be any smooth periodic NSE pair on an open positive-time interval.
Taking divergence, using div v=0 and commuting the actual smooth derivatives,
gives

  Delta p = -sum_{i,j} partial_i partial_j(v_i v_j).    (3.7)

The Fourier formula (3.4) solves this equation. The difference
p-R(v tensor v) is spatially harmonic. Its nonzero Fourier coefficients
vanish, so it is spatially constant. Hence

  p(t,x)-p(t,0) = Pi(v(t))(x).                          (3.8)

Thus equal local velocities imply equal normalized local pressures.
Pressure compatibility will be derived from velocity compatibility; it is
not an independent admission in Section 6.

For divergence-free v,

  F(v):=nu Delta v-P div(v tensor v)
       =nu Delta v-(v dot grad)v-grad Pi(v).           (3.9)

The sign follows from Delta Pi(v)=-div div(v tensor v) and
P=I-grad Delta_0^{-1} div.


4. SPATIAL C-INFINITY TRACE IMPLIES THE FULL SMOOTH INITIAL TRACE
--------------------------------------------------------------

LEMMA T_TRACE.

Suppose v,q are a smooth periodic NSE pair for t>0, with q(t,0)=0. Suppose
a is smooth and divergence-free and

  for every m>=0,
    lim_{t downarrow 0} ||v(t)-a||_{H^m}=0.            (4.1)

Then v(0)=a and q(0)=Pi(a) extend the pair jointly C-infinity to H x T3.
The exact NSE equation holds at t=0 with the right time derivative.

PROOF.

By (3.8), q(t)=Pi(v(t)) for positive t, and v_t=F(v).
Define polynomial spatial jet operators by

  J_0(b)=b,

  J_{n+1}(b)
    = nu Delta J_n(b)
      -sum_{j=0}^n binom(n,j)
          P div(J_j(b) tensor J_{n-j}(b)).              (4.2)

For every m>=2 and n>=0,

  J_n:H^{m+2n} -> H^m

is a continuous polynomial map. This follows by induction. For the Laplacian
term, apply the induction hypothesis with target order m+2. For a product
term, both factors are controlled in H^{m+1} by input H^{m+2n+2}; use (3.2),
then the bounded operator P div:H^{m+1}->H^m. All maps are finite polynomial
combinations of these continuous linear and bilinear operations.

Differentiating the already smooth positive-time PDE repeatedly and using
the product rule proves, by induction,

  partial_t^n v(t)=J_n(v(t)), t>0.                     (4.3)

This argument differentiates an existing trajectory. It asserts no ODE
existence theorem on a Frechet space with derivative loss.

By (4.1) and continuity of J_n,

  J_n(v(t)) -> J_n(a) in H^m as t downarrow 0.         (4.4)

Define W_n(t)=J_n(v(t)) for t>0 and W_n(0)=J_n(a). Each W_n is continuous
from H to H^m. For t>0 its derivative is W_{n+1}. For 0<eta<t, the Banach-
valued fundamental theorem of calculus gives

  W_n(t)-W_n(eta)=integral_eta^t W_{n+1}(s) ds.

Let eta decrease to zero. Continuity and boundedness on compact intervals
justify passage to the limit in H^m, giving

  W_n(t)-W_n(0)=integral_0^t W_{n+1}(s) ds.            (4.5)

Therefore W_n has right derivative W_{n+1}(0) at zero. All time derivatives
extend continuously in every H^m, with

  partial_t^n v(0+)=J_n(a).                            (4.6)

For pressure, define

  K_n(b)=sum_{j=0}^n binom(n,j)
                       Q(J_j(b),J_{n-j}(b)).          (4.7)

By continuity of Q and J_j, K_n:H^{m+2n}->H^m is a continuous polynomial
map for m>=2. The Leibniz rule gives partial_t^n q(t)=K_n(v(t)) for t>0.
These functions converge to K_n(a), and the same integral argument proves
smoothness of q in time through zero, with q(0)=Pi(a).

For each spatial derivative order r, choose m>r+3/2 in (3.3). All mixed
time-space derivatives consequently extend continuously to the closed
forward domain. Finite-order Taylor formulas along segments in a local
half-space chart, with continuous mixed derivatives and integral remainders,
give the corresponding within derivatives at boundary points. Applying this
at every finite order gives exactly joint C-infinity/ContDiffOn regularity
on the closed forward domain. A negative-time analytic extension is not used.

Finally, v_t(0+)=J_1(a)=F(a). Equations (3.9) and q(0)=Pi(a) give the NSE
identity at zero. The initial divergence condition is div a=0. QED.

The recurrence establishes existence of all initial time derivatives. It
establishes no factorial bound and no analyticity at t=0.

The chosen representatives consequently satisfy coherence (6.3). Joint
smoothness of u through zero and compactness of T3 imply convergence of
every spatial derivative uniformly as t downarrow 0; hence convergence in
every H^m. Therefore (6.4) also holds, and

  Phi(u,q) belongs to G_nu,rho(a).                    (8.3)

All positive centers are used. Selecting arbitrary metric collars only at
rational centers would not guarantee coverage, because their radii could
avoid an irrational time. A countable subcover may be selected only after
the full positive-time cover has been obtained.


9. EXACT GLOBAL INVERSE IDENTITIES
---------------------------------

9.1 Psi after Phi.

For t>0, (8.2) gives U_{Phi(u,q)}(t)=u(t). By (3.8) and q(t,0)=0,

  Q_{Phi(u,q)}(t)=Pi(u(t))=q(t).

Both pairs extend continuously through zero, with the same initial velocity
and normalized pressure. Thus

  Psi(Phi(u,q))=(u,q).                                (9.1)

9.2 Phi after Psi.

Fix gamma in G_nu,rho(a) and tau>0. A coherence representative g_tau of
gamma_tau has decoded velocity V_gamma on an open neighborhood of tau.
Its normalized pressure is Pi(V_gamma)=Q_gamma there. Hence its actual
decoded NSE germ is

  D_tau(gamma_tau)=[U_gamma,Q_gamma]_tau.              (9.2)

Applying the native local right inverse gives

  Phi(Psi(gamma))_tau
    =E_tau([U_gamma,Q_gamma]_tau)
    =E_tau(D_tau(gamma_tau))
    =gamma_tau.                                      (9.3)

This holds for every positive tau, so the germ families are equal.

THEOREM NSE_GR_POSITIVE_TIME_GERM_FAMILY_EQUIVALENCE.

  forall nu>0, forall rho!=0, forall a with Data(a),
    Phi:S_nu(a) ~= G_nu,rho(a),

  with inverse Psi and both identities (9.1), (9.3).

QED.

The proof does not require a separate global uniqueness theorem for NSE.
The inverse identities follow from exact recovery of the supplied trajectory
and the existing local geometric inverse. Empty carriers are permitted.


10. GLOBAL FEFFERMAN-B EXISTENCE EQUIVALENCE
------------------------------------------

Define the geometric existence proposition

  GRFamilyB(nu,rho) :=
    forall a, Data(a) -> Nonempty(G_nu,rho(a)).         (10.1)

This definition quantifies over the original full smooth periodic
divergence-free datum class, with arbitrary spatial mean.

By (1.2) and the theorem of Section 9, for every such a,

  (exists u,p, NSSolution(nu,a,u,p))
    <-> Nonempty(S_nu(a))
    <-> Nonempty(G_nu,rho(a)).                         (10.2)

Quantifying over the unchanged datum class gives

THEOREM NSE_B_IFF_GR_BOUNDARY_FAMILY.

  forall nu>0, forall rho!=0,
    FeffermanB(nu) <-> GRFamilyB(nu,rho).              (10.3)

QED.

The definition of FeffermanB is the literal one in
Fefferman_adapter/NSEMaxwell/Classical.lean.

The theorem establishes equivalence of the two universal existence
propositions. It establishes neither proposition independently.

The global GR symbol in (10.3) has the explicit family meaning (10.1).
Replacing it by an unrestricted Einstein-existence proposition or existence
of one globally glued vacuum metric would change the theorem.


11. INITIAL-TIME NONANALYTICITY IS ADMITTED
-----------------------------------------

Fix nu>0 and kappa=4pi^2 nu. For alpha in {1/2,1}, set

  u_alpha(t,x)
    =e_1 sum_{k>=1} exp(-k^alpha-kappa k^2 t) cos(2pi k x_2),
  p_alpha=0, t>=0.                                   (11.1)

Every polynomially weighted Fourier series converges at t=0. Thus all mixed
space/time derivatives converge uniformly on compact forward time slabs,
and (u_alpha,0) is jointly C-infinity through zero.

The field is divergence-free. Its convection vanishes because the velocity
points in e_1 while its scalar amplitude is independent of x_1. The Fourier
heat equation therefore gives exact unforced NSE for every t>=0.

For alpha=1, the initial datum extends holomorphically to the spatial strip
|Im x_2|<1/(2pi). For alpha=1/2, its Fourier coefficients have no positive
exponential decay rate, so its smooth periodic initial datum is not real
analytic. Both are jointly analytic for t>0.

At x=0,

  |partial_t^n u_{alpha,1}(0+,0)|/n!
    =kappa^n/n! sum_{k>=1} k^{2n} exp(-k^alpha)
    >=(4 kappa n/e^2)^n.                              (11.2)

The inequality selects k=2n, uses (2n)^alpha<=2n and n!<=n^n.
Hence the time Taylor radius at zero is zero for both trajectories.

Nevertheless, each trajectory belongs to S_nu(u_alpha(0)) and has the
spatial C-infinity initial trace required by (6.4). Section 9 therefore
assigns to it a coherent GR germ at every tau>0.

The extension neither imposes nor derives an analytic GR germ through t=0.
It is compatible with both analytic and nonanalytic smooth spatial data.
These trajectories satisfy global smooth NSE existence and do not refute
the literal FeffermanB proposition.


12. GEOMETRIC AND LOGICAL SCOPE
------------------------------

12.1 Locality retained in the bulk.

The boundary velocity is global on (0,infinity) x T3 and extends smoothly
to the initial slice. Each metric remains a germ on its own radial/time
collar. The complete spatial torus is retained in every collar.

12.2 Center normalization.

For every tau, the germ gamma_tau retains

  beta_tau(tau,x)=rho,
  partial_t beta_tau(tau,x)=0.

These are separate centerwise normalizations. If all chosen representatives
were restrictions of one marked metric on one common hypersurface, they
would additionally force beta(t,x)=rho for every positive t. The present
construction neither proves such an identity nor supplies metric transition
maps. Decoded-velocity coherence is weaker than metric equality on overlaps.

12.3 Germ families versus raw metric atlases.

The bijection is with a family of germ classes indexed by all positive
centers. It is not a bijection with raw lists of representatives: index
sets, radii, cover refinements, and extensions outside germ neighborhoods
would give redundant choices.

Because normalization depends on the center, these objects should not be
identified with the ordinary sheaf of one metric under unrestricted
restriction/re-centering. Their compatibility is the explicitly defined
decoded-boundary compatibility (6.3).

12.4 Foliation.

The coordinate t labels the selected boundary foliation. The construction
does not assert an absolute physical time. The chosen Gaussian marking,
flat induced boundary geometry, periodic spatial structure, constitutive
condition, and normalization remain part of the exact sector.

Covariance under arbitrary refoliations, a diffeomorphism quotient, global
hyperbolicity, geodesic completeness, and one global Einstein spacetime
are not conclusions of this theorem.

12.5 Quantifiers and regularity.

There is no uniform positive lower bound for all collar radii, and no
analyticity premise at the initial slice. The spatial initial trace is
convergence in every H^m, equivalently C-infinity. Replacing that trace by
L^2, distributional, or pointwise convergence would require a separate
theorem and is not performed here.

APPENDIX A. DIRECT POSITIVE-TIME JOINT ANALYTICITY PROOF
=====================================================

EXACT LEMMA

Let nu>0. Let u,p be an already supplied solution of unforced incompressible
NSE on [0,infinity) x T^3, jointly C-infinity up to t=0 and unit-periodic in
space. Define q(t,x)=p(t,x)-p(t,0). Then q is jointly C-infinity up to t=0,
q(t,0)=0, grad_x q=grad_x p, and u,q are jointly real analytic on
(0,infinity) x T^3. No restriction on the velocity mean is needed.

This is a conditional regularity theorem for a given smooth trajectory.
It proves neither arbitrary-data global existence nor initial-time analyticity.

The following Fourier fixed-point argument proves the lemma directly. It uses
only the Banach contraction theorem, elementary Fourier convolution, Duhamel's
formula, and uniform-limit holomorphy. It therefore avoids leaving the joint
analyticity conclusion as an unresolved literature premise at B-tier.

1. FOURIER BANACH SPACES

Write xi_k=2*pi*k, k in Z^3. For sigma>=0 define

  ||v||_{A_sigma} = sum_k exp(sigma*|xi_k|) |vhat(k)|.

Use the Euclidean norm on C^3 and the Frobenius norm on complex 3x3 tensors.
A_sigma denotes the corresponding weighted ell^1 Fourier space. For scalars,
vectors, and tensors the norm is interpreted in the relevant finite-dimensional
coefficient space. The triangle inequality |xi_{j+l}|<=|xi_j|+|xi_l| yields

  ||v tensor w||_{A_sigma} <= ||v||_{A_sigma} ||w||_{A_sigma}.

For k!=0 let P(k)=I-k tensor k/|k|^2; set P(0)=I. Each matrix has complex
Euclidean operator norm <=1. Consequently

  |(P div F)hat(k)| <= |xi_k| |Fhat(k)|,

with zero left-hand side for k=0. Keeping P(0)=I retains arbitrary mean.

Every smooth periodic field belongs to A_0. For example, C^4 control gives
|vhat(k)| <= C(1+|k|^2)^(-2)||v||_{C^4}, and the displayed weight is summable
on Z^3. Thus t -> u(t) is continuous into A_0 on every compact time interval.

2. COMPLEX-TIME SECTOR AND HEAT ESTIMATES

Fix 0<theta<pi/2 and omega>0. Set

  S_{theta,T} = {z in C : 0<|z|<T, |arg z|<theta},
  sigma(z)=omega*sqrt(nu*Re z),
  S(z)=exp(nu*z*Delta).

The heat multiplier is exp(-nu*z*|xi_k|^2). Completing the square gives

  ||S(z)b||_{A_{sigma(z)}} <= L ||b||_{A_0},
  L=exp(omega^2/4).

For z in S_{theta,T}, 0<s<1, put

  b0=nu*(1-s)*Re z > 0,
  d=sigma(z)-sigma(sz)
   =omega*sqrt(nu*Re z)*(1-sqrt(s)).

For y>=0,

  y exp(-b0*y^2+d*y)
  = [y exp(-(b0/2)*y^2)] [exp(-(b0/2)*y^2+d*y)]
  <= (e*b0)^(-1/2) exp(d^2/(2*b0)).

Moreover

  d^2/(2*b0)
  = (omega^2/2)*(1-sqrt(s))/(1+sqrt(s)) <= omega^2/2.

Hence

  ||S((1-s)z) P div F||_{A_{sigma(z)}}
  <= exp(omega^2/2) / sqrt(e*nu*(1-s)*Re z)
     * ||F||_{A_{sigma(sz)}}.

This estimate is the exact one-derivative smoothing needed for the nonlinear
term. It is uniform over the entire spatial torus.

3. ANALYTIC FIXED POINT

Let X consist of A_0-valued holomorphic maps on S_{theta,T}, continuous at
z=0 into A_0, with finite norm

  ||V||_X = sup_{z in S_{theta,T}} ||V(z)||_{A_{sigma(z)}}.

For completeness, an X-Cauchy sequence converges uniformly into A_0.
For each z, Fatou's inequality for its weighted Fourier sums preserves the
weighted estimates and gives convergence in the X norm. Holomorphy is
preserved on compact subsets. Continuity at zero and the initial-value
condition are preserved by uniform A_0 convergence. The affine ball is
nonempty because z -> S(z)b belongs to it. Work in the closed affine subset V(0)=b and the closed norm ball of
radius R. Define the bilinear map

  B(V,W)(z)
  = -z * integral_{s=0}^1 S((1-s)z) P div(V(sz) tensor W(sz)) ds.

The previous estimate and integral_0^1 (1-s)^(-1/2) ds=2 imply

  ||B(V,W)||_X <= C_{theta,omega} sqrt(T/nu) ||V||_X ||W||_X,

  C_{theta,omega}=2*exp(omega^2/2)/sqrt(e*cos(theta)).

The integral defines a holomorphic A_0-valued map: on each compact subset of
the sector, the integrand is holomorphic for 0<s<1 and dominated by a constant
times (1-s)^(-1/2). Truncation in s and uniform convergence justify holomorphy.
The same estimate shows B(V,W)(z)->0 in A_0 as z->0 inside the sector.

Choose any M>0 with ||b||_{A_0}<=M. Put

  R=2*L*M,
  T=nu/(64*C_{theta,omega}^2*L^2*M^2).

Then K=C_{theta,omega}sqrt(T/nu)=1/(8*L*M), so K*R=1/4. The map

  F(V)=S(z)b+B(V,V)

satisfies ||F(V)||_X<=R/2+R/4 and

Banach's theorem gives a unique fixed point in this ball. Divergence freedom
is preserved, because the initial Fourier coefficients are divergence-free
and the nonlinear term is projected by P. The zero Fourier coefficient stays
equal to bhat(0), so arbitrary velocity means are retained.

4. JOINT, NOT MERELY SEPARATE, ANALYTICITY

Fix a positive real z0<T. There is a complex disk D around z0, with closure
inside S_{theta,T}, and a sigma_*>0 such that sigma(z)>sigma_* for z in D.
For w in C^3 with |Im w|<sigma_*, define

  V(z,w)=sum_k Vhat(z,k) exp(i*xi_k dot w).

Each term is jointly holomorphic in (z,w). On every compact subset with a
positive margin from |Im w|=sigma_*, the Fourier series converges uniformly:
the weighted ell^1 norm bounds the tail, including an exponentially decaying
factor from the positive spatial margin. Hence V is jointly holomorphic on
D x {w : |Im w|<sigma_*}. Its restriction to real variables is jointly real
analytic, uniformly over the periodic real spatial slice.

This construction avoids the invalid inference that separate REAL analyticity
in space and time would by itself imply joint real analyticity.

5. IDENTIFICATION WITH THE GIVEN SMOOTH SOLUTION

At any starting time s0>=0, set b=u(s0). The given smooth periodic NSE
solution satisfies the projected Fourier equation

  d/dt uhat(k)+nu*|xi_k|^2 uhat(k)
    = -(P div(u tensor u))hat(k).

Pressure vanishes after applying P. Integration gives the same real-time
mild/Duhamel equation as the fixed point.

Uniqueness in C([0,T'];A_0), T'<T, identifies the two solutions. Indeed for
two solutions U,V with the same initial value and uniform A_0 norms <=M0,
the difference obeys

  ||U(t)-V(t)||_{A_0}
  <= (2*M0/sqrt(e*nu))
     * integral_0^t (t-s)^(-1/2)||U(s)-V(s)||_{A_0} ds.

Choose h>0 with (4*M0/sqrt(e*nu))*sqrt(h)<1. Taking the supremum on [0,h]

finitely many suffice on [0,T']. This is a full uniqueness argument, not an
assumption that the given smooth solution lies in the analytic weighted ball.

  M_tau=max(1, sup_{s in [tau/2,tau]} ||u(s)||_{A_0}),

6. ANALYTIC CANONICAL PRESSURE AND THE INITIAL TRACE

Taking divergence of NSE gives

  Delta p = -sum_{i,j} partial_i partial_j(u_i u_j).

Define the mean-zero pressure pi by Fourier coefficients

  pihat(0)=0,
  pihat(k)=-sum_{i,j} xi_{k,i}xi_{k,j}/|xi_k|^2
                    * (u_i u_j)hat(k), k!=0.

The coefficient contraction has Frobenius norm one, so

  ||pi||_{A_sigma} <= ||u tensor u||_{A_sigma} <= ||u||_{A_sigma}^2.

The same quadratic bounded map applies to the complex-time fixed point.
Thus pi is A_0-holomorphic with ||pi(z)||_{A_{sigma(z)}}<=R^2.
On every smaller disk D in the sector and for each fixed
0<sigma_*<inf_{z in D}sigma(z), its Fourier truncations converge locally
uniformly in the fixed Banach space A_{sigma_*}. Thus pi is holomorphic
into that fixed space and jointly analytic on the corresponding smaller
positive-time neighborhoods. Evaluation at x=0 is a
bounded functional on A_0. Therefore

  q(t,x)=pi(t,x)-pi(t,0)

is also jointly analytic there. For real t, p-pi is periodic harmonic and
hence spatially constant. Consequently

  q(t,x)=p(t,x)-p(t,0).

The latter expression defines q smoothly on the closed forward domain,
including t=0, directly from the supplied smooth p. It also proves q(t,0)=0
and grad_x q=grad_x p. Therefore u,q retain the original NSE equation at
t=0 with its right-time derivative convention.
