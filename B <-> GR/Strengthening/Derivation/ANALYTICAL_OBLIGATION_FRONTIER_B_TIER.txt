-------------------------------

Fix

  nu > 0,
  a : Field,

Set

  u := s.totalVelocity,
  p := s.totalPressure.

The existing endpoint

gives

  s.solution : NSSolution nu a u p.

It is sufficient to prove the following positive-center theorem:

  CENTER_ANALYTIC(nu,s,tau):

    0 < tau ->
    forall x0 : Vec,
      AnalyticAt Real
        (fun z : Real x Vec => u z.1 z.2)

Indeed,

  forall tau>0, forall x0, CENTER_ANALYTIC(nu,s,tau)

Thus the mathematical root is reduced to one supplied trajectory and one
arbitrary positive center. No global existence step occurs inside the analytic
proof.

------------------------------------

From s.solution:

3. FOURIER-WIENER SPACES
------------------------

Let

  Frequency := Fin 3 -> Int,
  xi_k := 2*pi*k in Real^3.

For sigma>=0 define

  A_sigma
  :=
  { c : Frequency -> Complex^3 :
      sum_k exp(sigma |xi_k|) |c(k)|_2 < infinity }.

Norm:

  ||c||_(A_sigma)
  :=
  sum_k exp(sigma |xi_k|) |c(k)|_2.

Tensor coefficients use the complex Frobenius norm.

W01. COMPLETENESS.

The map

  c(k) |-> exp(sigma |xi_k|) c(k)

is an isometry from A_sigma to vector-valued ell^1. Hence A_sigma is Banach.
For 0<=sigma0<=sigma1, the inclusion

  A_sigma1 -> A_sigma0

has norm at most one.

Mathlib base:
  lp.completeSpace;
  lp.evalCLM;
  lp.tsumCLM.

W02. SMOOTH PERIODIC SLICES.

For a smooth periodic field v, repeated integration by parts gives

  |vhat(k)|_2 <= C_N (1+|k|)^(-N).

Taking N>3 yields vhat in A_0. On every compact positive-time interval,
joint smoothness gives one summable majorant independent of time. Therefore

  t |-> uhat(t)

is continuous into A_0.

The existing endpoint

  uniform_datumFourierCoeff_moment_bound

supplies the required uniform polynomial Fourier majorants for every moment.

W03. ALGEBRA.

For convolution,

  (c*d)(k)=sum_l c(l)d(k-l),

and

  |xi_k| <= |xi_l|+|xi_(k-l)|.

Therefore

  ||c*d||_(A_sigma)
  <= ||c||_(A_sigma)||d||_(A_sigma),

and

  ||v tensor w||_(A_sigma)
  <= ||v||_(A_sigma)||w||_(A_sigma).

W04. LERAY SYMBOL.

For k!=0,

  P(k)=I-(k tensor k)/|k|^2;

for k=0,

  P(0)=I.

Then

  ||P(k)c||_2 <= ||c||_2,

and for a tensor coefficient F,

  |[P div F](k)|_2
  <= |xi_k| |F(k)|_F.

At k=0 the divergence multiplier is zero. Thus the nonlinearity has zero
constant mode, while the heat operator preserves the constant mode. The
velocity mean is unrestricted.

5. LEAN-FRIENDLY COMPLEX SECTOR
-------------------------------

For T>0 define

  Sector(T)
  :=
  { z : Complex :
      0 < Re z
      and |Im z| < Re z
      and |z| < T }.

This replaces the argument-function sector of Revision 1.

Properties:

  Sector(T) is open;
  z in Sector(T), 0<s<=1 -> s*z in Sector(T);
  z in Sector(T) -> |z| < 2 Re z;
  every real h with 0<h<T belongs to Sector(T).

Define

  sigma(z):=sqrt(nu Re z).

The use of Re z is legitimate because Re z>0 throughout the sector.


6. HEAT AND ONE-DERIVATIVE ESTIMATES
------------------------------------

Let y>=0 and a=nu Re z.

H01. HEAT.

Since

  sqrt(a)y-a y^2 <= 1/4,

we obtain

  ||H(z)b||_(A_(sigma(z)))
  <= L ||b||_(A_0),

  L:=exp(1/4).                                         (6.1)

H02. HEAT-LERAY-DIVERGENCE.

For 0<s<1 put

  a_s:=nu(1-s)Re z,

  d_s:=sigma(z)-sigma(sz)
      =sqrt(nu Re z)(1-sqrt(s)).

Then

  y exp(-a_s y^2+d_s y)
  <= [e a_s]^(-1/2) exp(d_s^2/(2a_s)),

and

  d_s^2/(2a_s)
  =(1-sqrt(s))/(2(1+sqrt(s)))
  <=1/2.

Hence the exponential factors cancel and

  ||H((1-s)z)P div F||_(A_(sigma(z)))
  <= [nu(1-s)Re z]^(-1/2)
       ||F||_(A_(sigma(sz))).                          (6.2)

This is the sole parabolic derivative-gain estimate needed by the nonlinear
fixed point.


7. COMPLETE SECTOR PATH SPACE
-----------------------------

For b in A_0 define X_T(b) as the set of maps U on Sector(T) union {0} such
that

  U(0)=b;
  U is continuous at 0 as an A_0-valued map;
  U is complex differentiable on Sector(T) as an A_0-valued map;
  ||U||_X:=sup_(z in Sector(T)) ||U(z)||_(A_(sigma(z))) < infinity.

Metric:

  d_X(U,V)
  :=
  sup_(z in Sector(T))
    ||U(z)-V(z)||_(A_(sigma(z))).

X01. COMPLETENESS.

Let U_n be d_X-Cauchy.

For each fixed z, U_n(z) is Cauchy in the Banach space A_(sigma(z)), giving
a pointwise limit U(z). Since every weighted norm dominates the A_0 norm,
U_n converges uniformly to U in A_0. Hence continuity at zero is preserved.

For n sufficiently large and every z,

  ||U_n(z)-U_m(z)||_(A_(sigma(z))) <= epsilon.

Letting m tend to infinity in the fixed complete space A_(sigma(z)) gives
the same bound with U(z), uniformly in z. Therefore U_n->U in d_X.

Local uniform A_0 convergence of complex differentiable maps on the open
sector preserves complex differentiability. Thus X_T(b) is complete. Every
closed d_X ball is complete.

Mathlib endpoint:

  TendstoLocallyUniformlyOn.differentiableOn.

No variable-weight holomorphy theorem is assumed. The weight enters only the
metric and pointwise Banach completeness.


8. DUHAMEL MAP
--------------

For U,V in X_T(b), define

  B(U,V)(z)
  :=
  -z integral_(s=0)^1
      H((1-s)z)P div(U(sz) tensor V(sz)) ds.            (8.1)

D01. NORM BOUND.

By W03, H02, |z|<2 Re z, Re z<=|z|, and

  integral_0^1 (1-s)^(-1/2) ds=2,

we obtain

  ||B(U,V)||_X
  <= 4 sqrt(T/nu) ||U||_X ||V||_X.                    (8.2)

D02. CONTINUITY AT ZERO.

The same calculation with |z| in place of T gives

  ||B(U,V)(z)||_(A_0)
  <= 4 sqrt(|z|/nu) ||U||_X||V||_X,

so B(U,V)(z)->0 as z->0 inside the sector.

D03. COMPLEX DIFFERENTIABILITY.

For epsilon>0 define the truncated map

  B_epsilon(U,V)(z)
  :=
  -z integral_0^(1-epsilon)
      H((1-s)z)P div(U(sz) tensor V(sz)) ds.

On the compact parameter interval [0,1-epsilon], the integrand is complex
differentiable in z and has locally uniform derivative bounds. Mathlib's
parametric integral derivative theorem gives complex differentiability of
B_epsilon.

For every compact K inside Sector(T),

  sup_(z in K)
  ||B(U,V)(z)-B_epsilon(U,V)(z)||_(A_0)
  <= C_K sqrt(epsilon)||U||_X||V||_X.

Thus B_epsilon converges locally uniformly to B as epsilon decreases to zero.
The locally uniform limit theorem gives complex differentiability of B.

This truncation-and-tail route replaces Revision 1's single improper
parametric-integral step and removes a hidden derivative-domination burden.


9. EXPLICIT CONTRACTION
-----------------------

Choose M with

  1 <= M,
  ||b||_(A_0) <= M.

Set

  L:=exp(1/4),
  R:=2LM,
  T:=nu/(1024 L^2 M^2),
  K:=4 sqrt(T/nu)=1/(8LM).

Then

  K R=1/4,
  2 K R=1/2.

Define

  Phi(U):=H(.)b+B(U,U).

On the closed radius-R ball in X_T(b),

  ||H(.)b||_X <= LM=R/2,

  ||B(U,U)||_X <= K R^2=R/4,

so

  ||Phi(U)||_X <=3R/4<R.

Moreover,

  ||Phi(U)-Phi(V)||_X
  <= K(||U||_X+||V||_X)||U-V||_X
  <=(1/2)||U-V||_X.

The heat path supplies a point in X_T(b), so the ball is nonempty.

Mathlib's

  ContractingWith.fixedPoint;
  ContractingWith.fixedPoint_isFixedPt;
  ContractingWith.fixedPoint_unique

produce the unique fixed point U_star in the ball:

  U_star(z)=H(z)b+B(U_star,U_star)(z).                 (9.1)

The fixed point remains spectrally divergence-free. Its constant coefficient
equals the constant coefficient of b.


10. REAL MILD UNIQUENESS
------------------------

Let U,V be A_0-continuous real mild solutions with the same initial value on
[0,T0]. Suppose

  ||U(t)||_(A_0), ||V(t)||_(A_0) <= M0.

The real heat estimate

  ||H(t-r)P div F||_(A_0)
  <=[nu(t-r)]^(-1/2)||F||_(A_0)

gives

  ||U(t)-V(t)||_(A_0)
  <=2M0/sqrt(nu)
      integral_0^t (t-r)^(-1/2)||U(r)-V(r)||_(A_0) dr.

For

  4M0 sqrt(h/nu)<1,

taking the supremum on [0,h] forces U=V there. Restarting at h and repeating
finitely many times proves equality on every compact common interval.

This is uniqueness only in the A_0 mild class containing the supplied smooth
trajectory and the constructed fixed point. No weak-solution uniqueness or
global regularity statement is used.

12. MIXED FACTORIAL BOUNDS
--------------------------

This section replaces Revision 1's direct multivariable holomorphic-limit
claim. Only one-complex-variable Mathlib is required.

Let h satisfy

  0<h<=T/4.

Define the closed complex disk

  D_big:=closedBall(h,h/4).

For z in D_big,

  Re z >=3h/4,
  |Im z|<=h/4<Re z,
  |z|<=5h/4<=5T/16<T.

Hence D_big is contained in Sector(T).

Set

  sigma_min:=sqrt(3nu h/4),
  sigma_1:=sigma_min/2,
  rho:=h/8.

M01. LOCAL A_(sigma_1)-VALUED HOLOMORPHY.

On D_big,

  sigma(z)>=sigma_min=2sigma_1.

Finite Fourier truncations of U_star are A_(sigma_1)-valued holomorphic maps.
For the tail outside a frequency ball of radius N,

  ||tail_N U_star(z)||_(A_(sigma_1))
  <= exp(-sigma_1 N) ||U_star(z)||_(A_(sigma(z)))
  <= R exp(-sigma_1 N).

Therefore the truncations converge locally uniformly in A_(sigma_1).
The locally uniform limit theorem gives an A_(sigma_1)-valued holomorphic
map on the interior of D_big.

M02. TIME DERIVATIVES.

For every real t with |t-h|<h/8, the complex circle of radius rho centered at
t lies in D_big. The Banach-valued Cauchy estimate yields

  ||partial_t^m U_star(t)||_(A_(sigma_1))
  <= R m! rho^(-m).                                   (12.1)

Mathlib endpoint:

  Complex.norm_iteratedDeriv_le_of_forall_mem_sphere_norm_le.

M03. SPATIAL DERIVATIVES.

For y>=0,

  y^r <= r! sigma_1^(-r) exp(sigma_1 y).

Consequently, for every spatial derivative word alpha of length r,

  ||partial_x^alpha partial_t^m u(t,x)||_2
  <= R m! r! rho^(-m) sigma_1^(-r).                   (12.2)

The estimate is uniform in x and in the stated real time interval.
Termwise differentiation is justified by the same summable exponential
majorant and the existing Fourier synthesis identities.

M04. TOTAL FRECHET DERIVATIVE.

Let n=m+r and put

  A:=max(rho^(-1),sigma_1^(-1)).

Expanding an n-fold directional derivative in the four coordinate directions
produces at most 4^n mixed coordinate terms. Since

  m! r! <= n!,

(12.2) gives on a real spacetime ball centered at (t_star+h,x0):

  ||D^n u|| <= R (4A)^n n!.                           (12.3)

The supplied trajectory is already C-infinity on this ball. Therefore the
native theorem

  NSEGR.Assembly.analyticAt_of_iteratedFDeriv_bound

applies and yields

  AnalyticAt Real
    (fun z : Real x Vec => u z.1 z.2)
    (t_star+h,x0).

This is a joint real-analytic conclusion. Separate real time and space
analyticity is not used.
