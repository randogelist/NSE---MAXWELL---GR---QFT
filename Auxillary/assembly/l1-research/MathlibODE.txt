0. RESULT

Verified native ODE results provide closed-interval existence, Lipschitz
dependence on the initial label, joint continuity in label and time, uniqueness,
and C^n regularity of each individual time trajectory. They do not, by their
stated conclusions, provide C^n or C-infinity joint initial-label/time regularity.
No matching native joint-smooth ODE flow theorem was verified in the searched
pinned sources. A search failure is not a proof of absence from every namespace.

The central new Lean obligation is the differentiability/smoothness of the
solution family with respect to its initial label. Its mathematical proof must
establish the variational equations by uniform remainder estimates; formal
differentiation of an as-yet-undifferentiated flow is circular.

Newly verified simplification: inverse smoothness can be proved entirely within
the closed slab. HasFDerivWithinAt.of_local_left_inverse, smoothness of inversion
of invertible continuous linear maps, and finite ContDiffOn induction remove
the need for a collar extension or an open inverse-function theorem in that
part of the proof. Forward joint smoothness can likewise be developed directly
within the slab. Thus the previous finite-reflection construction is a valid
alternative, not a mandatory L1 dependency.

Newly verified alternative: ContDiffAt.contDiffAt_implicitFunction supports
smooth dependence of a Banach-space Volterra equation. It is not a ready-made
ODE smooth-dependence theorem: path-space superposition smoothness, the Volterra
linearization and its invertibility remain native obligations.

3. WHOLE-SLAB EXISTENCE AND UNIQUENESS: NATIVE INPUTS SUFFICE

Spatial periodicity and compactness of I × [0,1]^3 yield B,K>=0 with
  ||u(t,x)|| <= B,
  ||D_x u(t,x)|| <= K,
for all (t,x)∈S. Spatial differentiation of translation identities transports
the derivative bound from the fundamental cell to all space. Each slice is
smooth on all of R^3 by ClassicalMechanics.finite_velocity_slice_smooth.
Mean value gives LipschitzWith K (u t) for each t∈I.

For arbitrary starting time s∈I and center x0 set, in IsPicardLindelof,
  tmin=0, tmax=T, t0=<s,hs>, r=1, a=B*T+1, L=B, spatial constant=K.
All constants a,r,L,K are nonnegative reals. The only time-radius condition is
  B*max(T-s,s) <= B*T = a-r.
There is no K*T<1 premise. The native proof uses a contracting iterate, so
the desired finite interval is handled directly, without maximal continuation.

MF02 supplies a trajectory for every label; MF03/MF04 supply uniform local
families near each label. Global classical choice is legitimate, but its local
regularity must be transported from those families using uniqueness. Choices
outside I are immaterial to the endpoint.

MF08 and MF09 on [s,T] and [0,s] prove uniqueness from arbitrary s∈I.
Their one-sided domains Ici t/Iic t are not global subsets of I. MF20 transfers
the derivative using the relevant local nhdsWithin inclusion. Interior initial
times can alternatively use ODE_solution_unique_of_mem_Icc (R04 below).

Let X(t;s,a) be the resulting unique trajectory on I. Then
  X(r;t,X(t;s,a))=X(r;s,a),   X(s;s,a)=a,
  ||X(t;s,a)-a||<=B|t-s|,
  ||X(t;s,a)-X(t;s,b)||<=exp(K|t-s|)||a-b||.
Backward estimates follow by time reversal or by the corresponding leftward
argument. Define eta(t,a)=X(t;0,a), rho(t,x)=X(0;t,x). The two inverse laws follow
from the two-time identity, before any differentiability of rho is used.

Periodicity gives X(t;s,a+e_i)=X(t;s,a)+e_i by ODE uniqueness.
The PL within derivative and MF21/MF22 produce the literal materialVelocity
equation. These steps require new adapters, not new analytic assumptions.

5. CORE MISSING NATIVE EDGE: JOINT FORWARD SMOOTHNESS

The main intended target, with no changed L1 premises, is:

PROPOSED_DECLARATION_NOT_COMPILED:
theorem smoothVelocityOn_of_periodic_flow
    {T : ℝ} (hT : 0 < T) {u eta : Velocity}
    (hu : SmoothVelocityOn T u)
    (hperiodic : ∀ t ∈ Icc (0 : ℝ) T, SpatialPeriodic (u t))
    (hinit : ∀ a : Vec, eta 0 a = a)
    (hode : ∀ t ∈ Icc (0 : ℝ) T, ∀ a : Vec,
      HasDerivWithinAt (fun s => eta s a) (u t (eta t a))
        (Icc (0 : ℝ) T) t) :
    SmoothVelocityOn T eta

Neither divergence-free nor det=1 is needed for this edge. Supplying an entire
trajectory family does not supply its label derivatives; those must be proved.

5.1 First variation, with no formal-differentiation assumption.
Set A(t,a)=D_x u(t,eta(t,a)). Construct the unique continuous operator solution
  F(t,a)=Id + integral_0^t A(s,a) F(s,a) ds.
A uniform small-interval contraction or the Volterra factorial-series estimate
constructs F on the whole finite slab. The existence theorem for bounded u
does not automatically apply to the linear field A*F on an arbitrarily large
ball: that field has linear growth. Use interval patching or a Volterra series
for this auxiliary equation, with explicit bound ||F(t,a)||<=exp(Kt).

For a label perturbation h let delta_h(t)=eta(t,a+h)-eta(t,a). The already-native
Gronwall estimate gives sup_I ||delta_h|| <= exp(KT)||h||.
Uniform continuity of D_x u on a compact neighborhood of the relevant
trajectories supplies a modulus omega(r)->0 and the uniform Taylor remainder
  ||u(t,z+d)-u(t,z)-D_xu(t,z)d|| <= omega(||d||)||d||.
Thus R_h(t)=delta_h(t)-F(t,a)h satisfies R_h(0)=0 and
  ||R_h'(t)|| <= K||R_h(t)|| +
                omega(exp(KT)||h||) exp(KT)||h||.
MF10 yields sup_I ||R_h||/||h|| -> 0. This proves the label HasFDerivAt,
uniformly in time, rather than assuming it. A comparison of the F equations
and uniform continuity of A gives joint continuity of F.

The time derivative is already the within ODE derivative. The integral equation
and uniform estimates yield the actual JOINT within derivative
  D eta(t,a)(tau,h) = tau*u(t,eta(t,a)) + F(t,a)h
on S. Separate partial derivatives without joint continuity do not suffice;
the increment split and uniform remainder prove the joint derivative.
MF33 with n=0 gives ContDiffOn R 1 eta S.

5.2 Higher variations.
For r>=2, introduce Y_r with zero initial value and the Volterra equation
  d_t Y_r = A Y_r +
    sum_{partitions P of {1,...,r}, |P|>=2}
      D_x^{|P|}u(t,eta)[ Y_{|B_1|},...,Y_{|B_|P||} ].
Set Y_1=F. Every inhomogeneous term at stage r involves lower Y_j.
Periodic compact-cell bounds give every finite spatial jet bound; trajectory
and variation bounds and Gronwall give uniform finite bounds for every Y_r.
Apply the same difference-quotient/remainder argument at each stage to prove
Y_r=D_a^r eta. This requires operator/multilinear-map norm estimates and
continuity in labels. Merely writing the recurrence is not its Lean proof.

Mixed time/label derivatives follow inductively from the ODE and these
variational equations, using within-time derivatives at 0 and T. The joint
derivative field at every finite stage is verified by the increment split.
MF33 then builds each finite ContDiffOn order on S; MF16 gives infinity.
This direct construction needs no exterior extension of u.

5.3 Banach implicit-function alternative, precisely delimited.
For a short interval J use the Banach space C(J,Vec), and set
  Q(a,z)(t)=z(t)-a-integral_s^t u(r,z(r)) dr.
On sufficiently small J, ||V||<1 for
  V(w)(t)=integral_s^t D_xu(r,z(r))w(r) dr,
so D_zQ=Id-V is invertible by a convergent Neumann series. The implicit-function
APIs R05--R08 provide a C^n map a -> z once ContDiffAt of Q is proved.
The proof of smoothness of the superposition map z -> (r -> u(r,z(r))) in the
sup norm still requires uniform spatial Taylor estimates and multilinear
operator construction. No matching C-infinity superposition endpoint was
verified in the searched Calculus/ContinuousMap sources.
Fixed-time evaluation is continuous linear and preserves label smoothness.
Variable-time evaluation C(J,Vec)×J -> Vec is not generally differentiable;
it cannot by itself supply joint time regularity. Use the ODE and joint jets,
or a stronger path-space norm with its own calculus setup.
The implicit theorem is ContDiffAt, not a within-set theorem. Applying it to
time as a parameter at slab endpoints requires separate endpoint work. Using
only the unrestricted initial label as parameter avoids that issue.
Hence the alternative is a genuine implementation strategy, not a discharge
of the smooth-dependence obligation by one library theorem.

6. NEW DIRECT CLOSED-SLAB INVERSE SMOOTHNESS PROOF

Assume Section 5 supplies SmoothVelocityOn eta and the determinant calculation
below supplies det(D_a eta)=1. Both inverse laws already hold by uniqueness.
Let H(t,a)=(t,eta(t,a)), G(t,x)=(t,rho(t,x)); both map S to S.

6.1 Inverse continuity without differentiating a starting-time parameter.
From the two-time law,
  rho(t,x)=rho(s,X(s;t,x)).
The fixed-s backward trajectory map has Lipschitz constant at most exp(KT).
Together with the displacement bound, this yields
  ||rho(t,x)-rho(s,y)|| <= exp(KT)*(||x-y||+B|t-s|).
Thus G is ContinuousOn S, including the boundary. MapsTo G S S plus this
continuity gives the exact Tendsto into nhdsWithin required by R09.

6.2 First inverse derivative on the same slab.
The derivative of H at (t,a) is the triangular continuous linear equivalence
  (tau,h) -> (tau, tau*u(t,eta(t,a))+F(t,a)h),
with inverse
  (tau,k) -> (tau, F(t,a)^(-1)*(k-tau*u(t,eta(t,a)))).
Invertibility follows from det(F)=1 and finite-dimensional linear algebra.
Apply HasFDerivWithinAt.of_local_left_inverse with f=H, g=G, s=t=S.
The required eventual equality H(G(z))=z holds on all S. The result is the
within derivative D G(z)=(D H(G(z)))^(-1), at both slab endpoints as well.

6.3 Higher inverse regularity without collar extension.
Write M=fderivWithin R H S. MF15 gives ContDiffOn R infinity M S.
Induct on n to prove ContDiffOn R n G S.
Base: contDiffOn_zero and Section 6.1.
Induction: if G is C^n, M∘G is C^n on S, because MapsTo G S S. At each point
this map takes an invertible value. R10 states that inversion of continuous
linear maps is ContDiffAt of every order at an invertible value. Composition
gives a C^n derivative field z -> inverse(M(G(z))). Section 6.2 supplies the
matching HasFDerivWithinAt. MF33 yields C^(n+1). MF16 gives C-infinity.
Projection onto the spatial coordinate yields SmoothVelocityOn T rho.

No two-sided smooth extension, local inverse construction, or smoothness in the
starting-time variable is used in this proof. A new within-set inverse-smoothness
adapter will still need to be implemented and externally audited.

7. DETERMINANT AND FINAL RECORD

Once the first variation is established, F'=D_xu(t,eta)F and F(0)=Id.
For arbitrary square F, including singular F, the polynomial determinant rule is
  (det F)'=trace(adj(F)*F').
For the project's 3x3 matrix this can be derived directly from MF25 and the
product rule, without invoking an unverified Mathlib determinant-calculus API.
Then MF26/MF27 give
  (det F)'=trace(F*adj(F)*D_xu)
          =det(F)*trace(D_xu)
          =det(F)*divergence(u)=0.
MF28 and det(F(0))=1 prove determinant_one. Inverse matrices are introduced only
after this step; no invertibility premise is smuggled into the determinant proof.
Coordinate realization of the continuous linear map F must match exactly
deformationGradient, whose i,j entry is coordinateDerivative (eta t) j a i.

The L1 record is then assembled from: forward and inverse smoothness; the two
inverse laws; lattice equivariance; identity initial map; determinant_one.
The flow equation is the already-proved within ODE, converted by MF21.
This is finite-slab particle-flow existence for a supplied smooth velocity.
It asserts neither existence of an NSE velocity nor global-time continuation.
