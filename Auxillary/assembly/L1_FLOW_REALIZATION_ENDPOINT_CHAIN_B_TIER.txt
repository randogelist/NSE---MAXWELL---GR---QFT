3. E03 -- ALL-START-TIME WHOLE-SLAB TRAJECTORIES
-----------------------------------------------

PREMISES: continuous u on S and E02 bounds B0,K.
OUTPUT: for every s in I and a in E, a path X(.;s,a) in B with
  X(s;s,a)=a,
  X(t;s,a)=a+integral_s^t u(r,X(r;s,a)) dr,
  HasDerivWithinAt (t |-> X(t;s,a)) (u(t,X(t;s,a))) I t for every t in I.

DERIVATION:
  Define gamma_0(t)=a and
    gamma_(n+1)(t)=a+integral_s^t u(r,gamma_n(r)) dr.
  By induction, in both time directions,
    ||gamma_(n+1)(t)-gamma_n(t)||
      <= B0 K^n |t-s|^(n+1)/(n+1)!.
  Base n=0 uses ||u||<=B0. The induction step uses the spatial Lipschitz
  bound and integrates |r-s|^(n+1) over the interval between s and t.
  The scalar series is summable uniformly on I, since |t-s|<=T.
  Hence gamma_n converges in the Banach space B to a continuous X.
  The Picard map is Lipschitz in the sup norm with constant KT, so the limit
  satisfies the integral equation even when KT>=1. The fundamental theorem
  of calculus for its continuous integrand gives the actual within derivative,
  including the right-sided derivative at 0 and left-sided derivative at T.

  Equivalent native entry:
    IsPicardLindelof parameters t0=s, initial radius=1,
      ambient radius=B0*T+1, norm bound=B0, Lipschitz bound=K.
    B0*max(T-s,s)<=B0*T=(B0*T+1)-1 verifies its radius condition.
  No KT<1 assumption, maximal continuation theorem, or time extension is needed.

LINKS: ODE.MF01/MF02/MF05/MF06; PicardLindelof and ExistUnique modules.

4. E04 -- UNIQUENESS, DISPLACEMENT, STABILITY
--------------------------------------------

PREMISES: E03; E02 bounds.
OUTPUT:
  each prescribed initial value at s determines one path on I;
  ||X(t;s,a)-a|| <= B0|t-s|;
  ||X(t;s,a)-X(t;s,b)|| <= exp(K|t-s|)||a-b||.

DERIVATION:
  Subtract two integral equations. For equal initial values, their continuous
  difference has a finite sup norm D and satisfies
    ||delta(t)|| <= K * integral_(between s,t) ||delta(r)|| dr.
  Iterating n times gives ||delta(t)||<=D K^n|t-s|^n/n! -> 0.
  For initial separation d0, the same iteration yields
    ||delta(t)|| <= d0*sum_(j<n)(K|t-s|)^j/j!
                    + D(K|t-s|)^n/n!;
  passage to the limit gives the exponential estimate. Displacement follows
  directly from the integral equation and ||u||<=B0.

  Consequently, for fixed s, (t,a) |-> X(t;s,a) is jointly continuous:
    ||X(t;s,a)-X(q;s,b)|| <= exp(KT)||a-b||+B0|t-q|.

7. E07 -- C-INFINITY SUPERPOSITION ON CONTINUOUS PATHS
------------------------------------------------------

PREMISES: E01/E02; B=C(I,E).
OUTPUT: N:B->B, N(gamma)(t):=u(t,gamma(t)), is C-infinity.
  For r>=0,
    D^r N(gamma)[h_1,...,h_r](t)
      = U_r(t,gamma(t))[h_1(t),...,h_r(t)],
    ||D^r N(gamma)|| <= M_r.

DERIVATION:
  Joint continuity of U_r and continuity of the paths imply that the displayed
  output is a continuous path. Multilinearity and the sup estimate give a
  bounded r-linear operator A_r(gamma):B^r->B of norm at most M_r.
  The E02 mean-value bound gives
    ||A_r(gamma+h)-A_r(gamma)|| <= M_(r+1)||h||_infinity.
  The E02 second-order remainder gives, in the operator norm on L^r(B;B),
    ||A_r(gamma+h)-A_r(gamma)
        - (h_1,...,h_r |-> A_(r+1)(gamma)[h,h_1,...,h_r])||
      <= (M_(r+2)/2)||h||_infinity^2.

  of A_r. Its derivative field is continuous by the preceding Lipschitz bound.
  Starting with A_0=N, induction proves N is C^m for every finite m, with the
  asserted D^r formula. Thus N is C-infinity on the entire Banach space B.

8. E08 -- VOLTERRA RESOLVENT ON THE WHOLE FINITE INTERVAL
--------------------------------------------------------

PREMISES: gamma in B; E01/E02.
DEFINITIONS:
  V f(t):=integral_0^t f(s) ds, a bounded linear map B->B with ||V||<=T.
  K_gamma h(t):=integral_0^t U_1(s,gamma(s))[h(s)] ds.
OUTPUT:
  ||K_gamma^n|| <= (KT)^n/n! for n>=0;
  R_gamma:=sum_(n>=0) K_gamma^n exists in operator norm;
  (Id-K_gamma)R_gamma=R_gamma(Id-K_gamma)=Id;
  ||R_gamma||<=exp(KT).

DERIVATION:
  E01 makes s |-> U_1(s,gamma(s)) continuous on I. Thus K_gamma maps continuous
  paths to continuous paths; E02 supplies its bounded linear operator estimate.
  For every h and t in I, induction on n gives
    ||K_gamma^n h(t)|| <= K^n t^n/n! * ||h||_infinity.
  Integrating the induction bound proves the next power; n=0 is the identity.
  Taking sup over t gives the operator bound. The bounded-operator space is
  Banach; comparison with the exponential series yields norm convergence.
  For R_N=sum_(n=0)^N K_gamma^n,
    (Id-K_gamma)R_N=R_N(Id-K_gamma)=Id-K_gamma^(N+1).
  The last power tends to zero by the factorial bound. Continuity of bounded
  operator composition gives both inverse identities and the norm estimate.
  In particular Id-K_gamma is a continuous linear equivalence.

  This argument remains valid when KT>=1 and when K=0. No smallness hypothesis
  or appeal to the ordinary norm<1 geometric-series criterion is made.

----------------------------------------------------

PREMISES: E09.

  J_r(t,a)[h_1,...,h_r]
    := (D^r Gamma(a)[h_1,...,h_r])(t).

  J_0(0,a)=a; J_1(0,a)=Id; J_r(0,a)=0 for r>=2.

DERIVATION:
  Evaluation ev_t:B->E is bounded linear, norm at most 1. For fixed t it

  remainder uniform over t in I.

  in its operator norm. Continuity of a |-> D^r Gamma(a) in the Banach operator
  norm then gives joint continuity in (t,a), including t=0,T.
  Evaluating Gamma(a)(0)=a at the fixed time 0 and differentiating in a yields
  the stated initial jets.

  asserted. That map on arbitrary C^0 paths is not the required smooth map.

11. E11 -- ACTUAL TIME EQUATIONS FOR ALL LABEL JETS
---------------------------------------------------

PREMISES: E07/E09/E10 and the Banach identity Gamma=c+V(N composed with Gamma).
DEFINITIONS:
  P_0(t,a):=u(t,J_0(t,a)).
  For r>=1, let Part(r) be the finite set of set partitions of {1,...,r}.
  For arguments h_1,...,h_r, set
    P_r(t,a)[h_1,...,h_r] :=
      sum_(pi in Part(r))
        U_(|pi|)(t,J_0(t,a))[
          J_(|B|)(t,a)[h_i : i in B] : B in pi ].
  Any fixed ordering of blocks and their members gives the same expression,
  by symmetry of the derivatives. Partitions carry no additional factorial.
OUTPUT:
  J_r(t,a)=J_r(0,a)+integral_0^t P_r(s,a) ds;
  HasDerivWithinAt (t |-> J_r(t,a)) (P_r(t,a)) I t.

DERIVATION:
  Differentiate the Banach identity in labels. The map V is bounded linear,
  so it commutes with Frechet differentiation. The iterated chain rule for
  N composed with Gamma gives exactly the finite partition formula above.
  That formula can also be checked inductively: differentiating U creates
  a new singleton block; differentiating a J factor inserts the new index
  into that block. Every partition of r+1 arises once by these alternatives.
  Fixed-time evaluation gives the displayed integral identity.
  Every P_r is jointly continuous by E01/E10 and finite multilinear operations.
  The continuous-integrand fundamental theorem gives the actual within time
  derivative, including both endpoints of I.

  In particular F:=J_1 satisfies the actual variation equation
    partial_t^I F(t,a)=U_1(t,eta(t,a)) composed with F(t,a), F(0,a)=Id.
  This is derived after label differentiability, not assumed by formal
  differentiation of an unknown family.

Optional quantitative jet bounds, derived by Gronwall:
  C_1:=exp(KT),
  C_r:=T exp(KT) * sum_(pi in Part(r), |pi|>=2)
                     M_(|pi|) product_(B in pi) C_(|B|), r>=2.
  Then sup_(t,a in S)||J_r(t,a)||<=C_r. In the sum, every block size is <r.
  The one-block term is U_1 J_r and is absorbed by the linear Gronwall factor.

12. E12 -- JOINT WITHIN DERIVATIVE OF EVERY JET
----------------------------------------------

PREMISES: E10/E11.
OUTPUT, for every r>=0 and z=(t,a) in S:
  HasFDerivWithinAt J_r L_r S z,
  L_r(t,a)(tau,h)=tau P_r(t,a)+J_(r+1)(t,a)[h, ...].
  The field L_r is jointly continuous on S.

DERIVATION:
  Let (t+tau,a+h) remain in S. Split the increment exactly as
    J_r(t+tau,a+h)-J_r(t,a)
     = integral_t^(t+tau) P_r(s,a+h) ds
       + J_r(t,a+h)-J_r(t,a).
  By continuity of P_r at (t,a), the first term minus tau P_r(t,a) has norm
    <= |tau| * epsilon(tau,h), with epsilon(tau,h)->0.
  The second term minus J_(r+1)(t,a)[h,...] is o(||h||), uniformly in t,
  by the Banach-space differentiability of D^r Gamma and ||ev_t||<=1.
  Therefore the full remainder is o(|tau|+||h||), equivalent to o(||(tau,h)||)
  for a product norm. This is the joint Frechet derivative within S.
  At t=0 or T the same split uses only allowed increments and oriented integrals.
  Continuity of L_r follows from continuity of P_r and J_(r+1).

13. E13 -- SIMULTANEOUS JOINT C-INFINITY BOOTSTRAP
-------------------------------------------------

PREMISES: E01/E10/E11/E12; UniqueDiffOn R S.
OUTPUT: forall r>=0, J_r is C-infinity on S; in particular SmoothVelocityOn T eta.

DERIVATION:
  For m>=0 define A_m := forall r>=0, ContDiffOn R m J_r S.
  A_0 follows from E10 and contDiffOn_zero.
  Assume A_m. The map H(t,a)=(t,J_0(t,a)) is C^m and maps S into S.
  Every U_k is C-infinity on S by E01, so U_k composed with H is C^m.
  Each P_r is a finite sum of continuous multilinear compositions of these
  maps and the J_j; consequently every P_r is C^m on S.
  Since A_m also includes J_(r+1), every derivative field
    L_r(t,a)(tau,h)=tau P_r(t,a)+J_(r+1)(t,a)[h,...]
  is C^m as a map into the appropriate continuous-linear-map space.
  E12 supplies its exact within derivative relation. The successor theorem
    contDiffOn_succ_iff_hasFDerivWithinAt_of_uniqueDiffOn
  gives ContDiffOn R (m+1) J_r S for every r. Its analytic side condition is
  vacuous at a finite natural order m. Thus A_m implies A_(m+1).
  Induction gives every A_m; contDiffOn_infty gives C-infinity for every J_r.

  This is simultaneous induction in the regularity order m, with all label
  orders r available from E09 at the base. It neither assumes J_(r+1) already
  has order m+1 nor circularly differentiates an unproved label dependence.
  No reflection, collar extension, time-parameter implicit theorem or analytic
  regularity of u is required.

14. E14 -- UNIT JACOBIAN FROM THE ACTUAL VARIATION EQUATION

  closed-interval zero-derivative constancy theorem, gives J(t,a)=J(0,a)=1.

17. E17 -- JOINT C-INFINITY INVERSE
-----------------------------------

PREMISES: E13/E15/E16; UniqueDiffOn R S.
OUTPUT: SmoothVelocityOn T rho.

DERIVATION:

  ContDiffOn.fderivWithin gives M C-infinity on S.
  Prove Q is C^m on S by induction. Base m=0 is E15.
  If Q is C^m and maps S into S, then M composed with Q is C^m.
  Its values are invertible by E16. Inversion of continuous linear maps is
  C-infinity at every invertible value, so
    z |-> inverse(M(Q(z)))
  is C^m within S. E16 identifies this field with the actual derivative of Q.
  The within-set successor theorem proves Q C^(m+1).
  All finite orders imply Q C-infinity; spatial projection gives rho C-infinity.
