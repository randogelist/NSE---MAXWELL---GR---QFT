THEOREM:

  !  forall nu>0, forall c>0,
       MaxwellB(nu,c) -> FeffermanB(nu).

LOGICAL_SCOPE:
  The theorem transfers the specified global constrained-Maxwell existence

1. EXACT OBJECTS
---------------

V=EuclideanSpace R (Fin 3), Q=[0,1]^3, S=[0,infinity) x V.
The volume is standard Euclidean Lebesgue measure, vol(Q)=1, and
<f>=integral_Q f. Spatial fields have period one in every coordinate.
Joint smoothness means ContDiffOn R infinity on S. Time derivatives at
zero are the actual derivatives within [0,infinity).

Data(a): a is smooth, periodic, and div a=0. Its mean is unrestricted.
MagneticData(B0): Data(B0) and <B0>=0.
MagneticPath(B): B is jointly smooth on S and, for t>=0, periodic,
divergence-free and mean-zero.

The frozen scalar/vector inverse Laplacian is

  Lf(x)=sum'_k lambda(k) integral_Q cos(2*pi*k.(x-y)) f(y) dy,
  lambda(0)=0,
  lambda(k)=1/(4*pi^2*|k|^2) for k!=0.

Define

  KB=curl(LB),
  U_m[B]=m+KB,
  G[B]=B cross U_m[B]+nu curl B,
  V[B]=-curl G[B],
  Q[B]=div G[B],
  h[B]=L(Q[B]),
  DG[B](b)=b cross U_m[B]+B cross Kb+nu curl b,
  J[B]=c^2 curl B-DG[B](V[B]).

MaxwellB(nu,c) states that every m and MagneticData(B0) admit jointly
smooth spatially periodic B,E,rho,J on S satisfying B(0)=B0, <B(t)>=0,

  div B=0,     B_t+curl E=0,
  div E=rho,   E_t-c^2 curl B=-J,

and the exact identities E=G[B], rho=Q[B], J=J[B].

FeffermanB(nu) states that every Data(a) has jointly smooth spatially
periodic u,p on S satisfying

  div u=0,
  u_t+(u.grad)u+grad p=nu Delta u,
  u(0)=a.

The pressure is periodic; a spatially constant gauge is admissible.

2. SHARED FOURIER/HODGE RESULTS
------------------------------

Reference: ../FOURIER_HODGE_COMPOSITION_B_TIER.txt, C01-C12.

The shared derivation proves, for the exact cosine-integral definitions,

  -Delta Lf=f-<f>,
  partial_j Lf=L(partial_j f),
  <Lf>=0,
  K(curl F)=F-<F>+grad L(div F).

The proof uses actual cube/torus coefficient transport, periodic integration
by parts, polynomial Fourier decay, rank-three lattice summability, and
normally convergent synthesis. Thus every identity is obtained on the
original smooth periodic class, without a replacement inverse operator.

The resulting reconstruction identities are

  U_<a>[curl a]=a  for Data(a),                        (H1)
  curl(U_m[B])=B  for MagneticData(B),                 (H2)
  div(U_m[B])=0,  <U_m[B]>=m.

For every MagneticPath B, U_m[B(t)] and the canonical pressure below are
jointly smooth on S, and div U_m[B(t)]=0.              (H3)

For every MagneticData B,

  G[B]+grad h[B]=K(curl G[B]).                         (H4)

The parameter theorem establishes D_t K(B(t))=K(D_t B(t)) on S.
Its boundary argument uses continuous Fourier jets and the derivative-
extension endpoint on the closure of the positive-time half-space. No
analyticity, negative-time smooth extension, or global uniform time bound
is part of these premises.

3. R01: MAGNETIC INITIAL DATA FOR ARBITRARY VELOCITY MEAN
--------------------------------------------------------

Assume MaxwellB(nu,c). Fix an arbitrary Data(a). Define

  m=<a>,  B0=curl a.

Smoothness and periodicity pass to actual spatial derivatives. Equality
of mixed partials gives div B0=0. The integral of each periodic derivative
over the cube vanishes, so <B0>=0. Hence MagneticData(B0).

Apply the assumed Maxwell existence proposition to this pair (m,B0).
Obtain B,E,rho,J satisfying the full frozen MaxwellSolution predicate.
Its magnetic smoothness, periodicity, Gauss law, and mean-zero condition
give MagneticPath(B).

4. R02: CANONICAL VELOCITY, PRESSURE, AND REGULARITY
--------------------------------------------------

Define

  u(t)=U_m[B(t)],
  h(t)=L(div G[B(t)]),
  e(t,x)=|u(t,x)|^2/2,
  p(t,x)=h(t,x)-e(t,x)+<e(t,.)>.

H3 gives joint smoothness on S and div u=0. Spatial periodicity follows
from the integer-character Fourier formula and periodic differentiation.
The mean term depends only on time. Since <h>=0 and vol(Q)=1,

  <p(t)>=0.

Thus p is the exact frozen canonical pressure, including its scalar gauge:

  p=L(div G)-|U_m[B]|^2/2+<|U_m[B]|^2>/2.

Joint smoothness of the actual mean term follows by differentiating its
cube integral on compact time slabs, including at zero. This is the k=0
coefficient instance of the same parameter argument. No additional pressure
regularity assumption is introduced.

By H2, curl u=B. The initial condition and H1 give

  u(0)=U_m[B(0)]=U_<a>[curl a]=a.

The harmonic mode m=<a> remains arbitrary throughout.

5. R03: ELECTRIC HODGE WITH THE REQUIRED ZERO MODE
------------------------------------------------

For a fixed nonnegative time, H2 and div u=0 imply

  B cross u=(u.grad)u-grad e.

Periodic integration gives mean convection zero, mean gradient zero, and
mean curl zero. Explicitly, for each i,

  integral_Q (u.grad)u_i
    =sum_j integral_Q partial_j(u_j*u_i)
      -integral_Q u_i*div u
    =0.

Therefore

  <G[B]>=<B cross u+nu curl B>=0.

Substitution in the general periodic Hodge identity proves exactly

  G[B]+grad L(div G[B])=K(curl G[B]).

The plus sign and zero-mode condition are essential. The full Leray
projection retains a constant Fourier coefficient; K curl removes it.
Their equality here follows from the derived vanishing mean of G.

The scalar potential itself has coefficients

  hhat(0)=0,
  hhat(k)=i*(k.Ghat(k))/(2*pi*|k|^2) for k!=0.

Thus the frozen scalarL potential, rather than only a pressure gradient
up to an unspecified gauge, is identified.

6. R04: FARADAY GIVES THE RECONSTRUCTED TIME DERIVATIVE
-----------------------------------------------------

The Maxwell Faraday law and the electric constitutive identity give

  B_t=-curl E=-curl G[B].

The parameter-smoothness theorem gives the actual within time derivative

  u_t=K(B_t).

Negation commutes with K on these smooth periodic fields. Consequently

  u_t=-K(curl G[B])=-G[B]-grad h,                      (M)

where the electric Hodge identity gives the final equality.

This calculation requires no off-solution additivity assertion for arbitrary
totalized Fourier sums. The existing Lean construction uses the concrete
K-negation identity and on-solution Faraday cancellation.

7. R05: RECOVERY OF THE NSE MOMENTUM EQUATION
-------------------------------------------

Since curl u=B and div u=0, the curl-curl identity yields

  curl B=grad div u-Delta u=-Delta u.

The Lamb identity gives

  (u.grad)u=B cross u+grad e.

The spatial gradient of <e(t,.)> is zero. Using (M) and the definition of p,

  u_t+(u.grad)u+grad p
    =-G-grad h+(B cross u+grad e)+(grad h-grad e)
    =-G+B cross u
    =-nu curl B
    =nu Delta u.

Thus the actual momentum equation holds for every t>=0 and every x,
including the right-time derivative at zero. Combined with R02, this is
an NSSolution(nu,a,u,p).

8. R06: EXISTENTIAL AND UNIVERSAL ASSEMBLY
----------------------------------------

The datum a was arbitrary in Data. Therefore FeffermanB(nu). Restricting
the parameters to the frozen positive range gives

  !  forall nu>0, forall c>0,
       MaxwellB(nu,c) -> FeffermanB(nu).

The proof uses the magnetic regularity, magnetic Gauss and mean constraints,
Faraday, and the prescribed electric constitutive field from the supplied
MaxwellSolution. The additional electric Gauss, Ampere, charge and current
requirements remain part of the full target Maxwell existence proposition;
the implication does not replace that proposition by a weaker definition.
