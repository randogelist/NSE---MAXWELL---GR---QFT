0. EXACT SCOPE AND DEFINITIONS
-----------------------------

Let V = EuclideanSpace R (Fin 3), Q = [0,1]^3, and
S = [0,infinity) x V. Volume is the standard Euclidean Lebesgue measure.
The volume of Q is one. Write <f> = integral_Q f.

All spatially periodic fields have period one in each coordinate. All spatial
derivatives are actual Frechet derivatives in the standard coordinate frame.
Time derivatives at t=0 are derivatives within [0,infinity). Joint smoothness
means exactly ContDiffOn R infinity on S. No analytic regularity, uniform
bound over the entire time half-line, or smooth negative-time continuation
is assumed. Total functions outside S may have arbitrary values.

Data(a) means that a:V->V is smooth, spatially periodic, and div a=0.
MagneticData(B) means Data(B) and <B>=0. There is no condition <a>=0.
MagneticPath(B) means joint smoothness on S, and, for every t>=0,
spatial periodicity, div B(t)=0, and <B(t)>=0.

For k in Z^3, put |k|^2=sum_j k_j^2 and define

  lambda(0)=0,
  lambda(k)=1/(4*pi^2*|k|^2)                         (k != 0),

  Lf(x)=sum'_k lambda(k)
          integral_Q cos(2*pi*k.(x-y)) f(y) dy.

For real scalar f this is precisely Classical.scalarL. Componentwise for
real vector f it is precisely Classical.vectorL. The vector definition uses
the real Bochner integral and scalar multiplication. These are the frozen
total definitions; convergence and every identity used below are proved on
the stated smooth periodic class.

Define the frozen operators

  K B = curl(LB),
  U_m[B] = m + K B,
  G_nu,m[B] = B cross U_m[B] + nu curl B,
  V_nu,m[B] = -curl(G_nu,m[B]),
  Q_nu,m[B] = div(G_nu,m[B]),
  h_nu,m[B] = L(Q_nu,m[B]),

  DG_nu,m[B](b)
    = b cross U_m[B] + B cross Kb + nu curl b,

  J_nu,c,m[B] = c^2 curl B - DG_nu,m[B](V_nu,m[B]),

  p_nu,m[B](x)
    = h_nu,m[B](x) - |U_m[B](x)|^2/2
      + <|U_m[B]|^2>/2.

The exact four-field target is

  H1: Data(a) -> U_<a>[curl a] = a.

  H2: MagneticData(B) -> curl(U_m[B]) = B, for every m.

  H3: MagneticPath(B) -> CanonicalSmoothness(nu,c,m,B),
      for every real nu,c and every m.

      CanonicalSmoothness consists of:
        joint smoothness of U_m[B(t)] on S;
        joint smoothness of p_nu,m[B(t)] on S;
        div(U_m[B(t)])=0 at every t>=0.

  H4: MagneticData(B) ->
        G_nu,m[B] + grad(h_nu,m[B]) = K(curl(G_nu,m[B])),
      for every real nu and every m.

These are the literal fields of NSEMaxwell.Bridge.FourierHodgeLaws.

FeffermanB(nu) is the frozen existence statement: every Data(a) admits a
globally defined jointly smooth spatially periodic velocity u and periodic
pressure p on S, with u(0)=a, div u=0, and

  u_t + (u.grad)u + grad p = nu Delta u.                (NS)

MaxwellB(nu,c) is the frozen constrained-Maxwell existence statement: for
every m and MagneticData(B0), jointly smooth spatially periodic fields
B,E,rho,J exist on S, with B(0)=B0, <B(t)>=0, and

  div B=0,       B_t+curl E=0,
  div E=rho,     E_t-c^2 curl B=-J,                    (MX)

subject to the exact constitutive identities

  E=G_nu,m[B],  rho=Q_nu,m[B],  J=J_nu,c,m[B].

The theorem is an equivalence of these two existence propositions. It does
not assert either global existence proposition separately.

2. C01: CUBE, TORUS, AND COORDINATE MEASURE
-----------------------------------------

Let f be a smooth spatially periodic scalar component. Unit coordinate
periodicity implies integer-translation periodicity: substitution at x-e_j
gives negative unit translations, and induction gives every integer shift.
Thus f descends to a continuous function on T^3=(R/Z)^3. Continuity follows
from the quotient topology. Complexify the scalar values when necessary.

The standard coordinate basis of V is orthonormal, so Euclidean Lebesgue
measure becomes product Lebesgue measure in these coordinates. The coordinate
linear bijection V -> (Fin 3 -> R) is measure preserving for these measures.
The default Pi norm is the maximum norm, so no isometry to that norm is used;
norm equivalence suffices whenever norms occur.

The closed cube [0,1]^3 and the half-open cube (0,1]^3 differ only on finitely
many coordinate hyperplanes. Each hyperplane is null by Fubini and the nullity
of real singletons. Hence the integrals below agree on these domains.

Define

  e_k(x)=exp(2*pi*i*k.x),
  fhat(k)=integral_Q e_{-k}(y) f(y) dy.

The product torus character equals e_k because a product of the coordinate
exponentials is the exponential of their sum. M01 with fundamental-domain
base point zero therefore identifies fhat with the Mathlib torus coefficient.
This proves the mathematical adapter corresponding to T01 for the exact
frozen aliases, actual volume, and actual cube.


3. C02: DERIVATIVE COEFFICIENTS AND RAPID DECAY
--------------------------------------------

For each coordinate j, integration by parts and Fubini give

  (partial_j f)^hat(k) = 2*pi*i*k_j*fhat(k).             (3.1)

The boundary terms cancel because f and e_{-k} have equal values on opposite
faces. The integrands are continuous on the compact cube. Differentiating
the spatial periodicity identity shows that all derivatives of f are also
periodic. Iteration proves

  (partial^alpha f)^hat(k)=(2*pi*i*k)^alpha*fhat(k).     (3.2)

These are the actual-derivative statements represented by T02.

For each integer q>=0, set

  A_q(f)=integral_Q |(1-Delta)^q f| < infinity.

Equation (3.2), linearity, and the binomial expansion imply

  ((1-Delta)^q f)^hat(k)
      =(1+4*pi^2*|k|^2)^q*fhat(k).

Taking norms in the integral gives the explicit bound

  |fhat(k)| <= A_q(f)*(1+4*pi^2*|k|^2)^(-q).           (3.3)

This includes k=0. Finiteness of A_q follows from the original smoothness
hypothesis and compactness; rapid decay is not a new assumption.

T03 provides a reusable coordinate-power alternative. Its decay denominator
is 1+sum_j(2*pi*|k_j|)^m, with finite constant C_m(f) assembled from L1 norms
of f and its m-th coordinate derivatives. For a scalar f, apply the source
vector theorem to the smooth periodic embedding x -> (f(x),0,0) and take
component zero; its signature requires no divergence-free condition.
For k!=0, some j satisfies
|k_j|>=|k|/sqrt(3); hence that endpoint yields

  |fhat(k)| <= C_m(f)*(sqrt(3)/(2*pi))^m*|k|^(-m).

For vector-valued f, apply these estimates to the three components and add
the component bounds. Both routes prove arbitrary polynomial decay directly
from Data-level regularity. No all-order Sobolev hypothesis is retained.


4. C03: WEIGHTED ABSOLUTE SUMMABILITY
------------------------------------

For n>=1, the number of lattice points with max_j |k_j|=n is

  (2n+1)^3-(2n-1)^3=24*n^2+2 <= 26*n^2.

Since |k|>=max_j |k_j|, comparison with sum_n n^(2-s) proves

  sum_k (1+|k|)^(-s) < infinity                       (s>3).

M05 is the public formal endpoint for this lattice fact. Its use requires
the standard embedding of Z^3 as a discrete lattice in V, rank three, and
separation of the zero term. T10 records the corresponding implementation on
the Pi coordinate space with its maximum norm; the stated norm comparison
transports its summability bound to the Euclidean norm.

For every integer r>=0, choose q with 2*q>r+3 in (3.3). Comparison proves

  sum_k (1+|k|)^r*|fhat(k)| < infinity.                (4.1)

Alternatively choose m=r+4 in the T03 bound; for k!=0, |k|>=1, so
(1+|k|)^r<=2^r*|k|^r and the summand is bounded by a constant times |k|^-4.
The zero coefficient is a single finite term.

The norm of the real-to-complex phase functional x -> 2*pi*i*k.x is
2*pi*|k| in Euclidean coordinates. Thus (4.1) supplies the literal
RapidScalarFourier hypothesis of T04 and the weaker absolute-summability
hypothesis of T05. If a Pi maximum norm appears in an adapter, finite-
dimensional norm equivalence changes constants only.


5. C04: INVERSION AND COEFFICIENT UNIQUENESS
-----------------------------------------

The descended torus function is continuous, and (4.1) with r=0 gives absolute
summability of its Fourier coefficients. M02 and the C01 coefficient adapter
therefore give, for every x,

  f(x)=sum_k e_k(x)*fhat(k).                            (5.1)

The bound |e_k(x)|=1 also proves uniform absolute convergence on all V.

An alternative equality route uses M12 to identify the continuous torus
coefficients with those of its L2 representative, then M03 and injectivity

source-port implementation for real periodic vector fields. This alternative
requires no l1 premise merely to infer equality from equal coefficients.


6. C05: IDENTIFICATION OF THE EXACT COSINE OPERATOR
-------------------------------------------------

Suppose f is real-valued. Complex conjugation commutes with its finite
coefficient integral, giving

  fhat(-k)=conjugate(fhat(k)).                          (6.1)

For every k,x, before any infinite summation,

  integral_Q cos(2*pi*k.(x-y))*f(y) dy
       = Re(e_k(x)*fhat(k)).                           (6.2)

The multiplier lambda is bounded on the integer lattice: it vanishes at zero
and is at most 1/(4*pi^2) otherwise. Thus (4.1) proves absolute convergence
of sum_k lambda(k)*e_k(x)*fhat(k). Continuous linearity of real part gives

  Lf(x)=Re(sum_k lambda(k)*e_k(x)*fhat(k)).             (6.3)

Conjugating the sum and reindexing by k -> -k leaves it unchanged, using
(6.1), e_{-k}=conjugate(e_k), and lambda(-k)=lambda(k). The complex sum is
therefore real. After its identification with a real number,

  Lf(x)=sum_k lambda(k)*e_k(x)*fhat(k).                 (6.4)

The frozen sum includes all k in Z^3. A nonzero pair {k,-k} contributes
2*Re(lambda(k)*e_k*fhat(k)) to both sides. There is no additional factor two.

The argument applies componentwise to the actual vector Bochner integral.
Consequently vectorL agrees with the real synthesis of the vector sequence
k -> lambda(k)*fhat(k). This is equality with the frozen definition, rather
than a replacement of that definition by an abstract inverse operator.


7. C06: SMOOTHNESS, POISSON, AND COMMUTATION
-----------------------------------------

For every spatial multi-index alpha,

  |partial^alpha[lambda(k)*e_k(x)*fhat(k)]|
    <= lambda(k)*(2*pi*|k|)^|alpha|*|fhat(k)|,

with zero contribution at k=0. Equation (4.1) gives a summable majorant,
uniformly in x, for every derivative order. The same bound holds for the
iterated Frechet derivative, up to a fixed finite-dimensional constant.
M07 yields spatial smoothness. M06, successively applied to the derivative
series, justifies termwise differentiation of every order. T04 and T09
provide source-port alternatives to these concrete series calculations.

Uniform absolute convergence on Q permits termwise integration. The product
of the three one-dimensional integer exponential integrals gives

  integral_Q e_k = 1 if k=0, and 0 otherwise.

Thus character orthogonality proves

  (Lf)^hat(k)=lambda(k)*fhat(k),
  <Lf>=0.                                              (7.1)

This is T05 at the scalar level and T08 after conjugate-symmetry transport
at the real-vector level.

Since -Delta e_k=4*pi^2*|k|^2*e_k, termwise differentiation and (5.1) give

  -Delta Lf=f-<f>.                                     (7.2)

For every j, expansions (3.1) and (6.4) have identical differentiated
coefficients; therefore

  partial_j Lf=L(partial_j f).                          (7.3)

Consequently, with scalar/vector operators distinguished by their codomains,

  div(LF)=L(div F),
  curl(LF)=L(curl F),
  grad(Lf)=L(grad f).                                  (7.4)

Linearity follows from linearity of the integrals and addition/scaling of
absolutely summable series. Every invocation is within the smooth periodic
class. No linearity claim for arbitrary totalized nonsummable series is used.
Periodicity follows termwise from the integer characters, or from (6.2).


8. C07: GENERAL HODGE IDENTITY; H1 AND H2
---------------------------------------

For every smooth periodic vector F, equality of mixed partials gives
div(curl LF)=0. Integrating each coordinate derivative of a periodic field
over Q gives zero, so <curl LF>=0. Thus

  div(KF)=0,  <KF>=0.

Using (7.2), (7.4), and the concrete curl-curl identity,

  K(curl F)
    = curl(curl(LF))
    = grad(div(LF))-Delta(LF)
    = grad(L(div F))+F-<F>.                            (8.1)

The same calculation yields curl(KF) equal to this expression. Equivalently,

  F=<F>+K(curl F)-grad(L(div F)).                       (8.2)

The harmonic component <F> is present explicitly.

If Data(a), then div a=0 and L0=0, so (8.1) yields

  U_<a>[curl a]=<a>+a-<a>=a.                           (H1)

If MagneticData(B), then div B=0 and <B>=0. Therefore, for every m,

  curl(U_m[B])=curl(m)+curl(KB)=B,                     (H2)
  div(U_m[B])=0,
  <U_m[B]>=m.                                         (8.3)

The reconstructed field is smooth and periodic. These conclusions retain
arbitrary m and impose no velocity mean-zero restriction.


9. C08: TIME-DEPENDENT COEFFICIENTS AND UNIFORM DECAY
--------------------------------------------------

A stronger parameter theorem suffices for H3:

  If F is jointly C-infinity on S and spatially periodic for t>=0, then
  (t,x) -> L(F(t,.))(x) is jointly C-infinity on S.      (PL)

The scalar and vector statements require neither div F=0 nor <F>=0.

The half-space S has UniqueDiffOn R. Iterating within Frechet derivatives
of F gives continuous smooth directional jets on S. Restricting spatial
directions gives ordinary spatial derivatives of the slices; restricting
the temporal direction gives the actual right time derivatives at zero.
Write F_r=D_t^r F. The project endpoints directional_smooth,
spatial_restriction_fderiv, and time_restriction_hasDerivWithinAt implement
these restrictions for the exact domain.

All spatial derivatives of all F_r are continuous on S. They are periodic:
differentiate the translation identities at positive time, then pass to
zero by continuity. Mixed derivatives commute in the positive-time interior
by Schwarz's theorem and at zero by continuity from that interior. Their
boundary values are therefore unambiguous within derivatives.

For each scalar component define

  b_r(t,k)=integral_Q e_{-k}(y)*F_r(t,y) dy.

For finite T>0, F_r and F_(r+1) are bounded on [0,T] x Q. The time
mean-value inequality gives a Lipschitz bound C*|s-t| uniformly in y.
The character has modulus one. A constant majorant is integrable over Q;
the existing project endpoint

  NSEMaxwell.DominatedWithin.hasDerivWithinAt_integral_of_dominated

therefore gives, including at zero,

  D_t b_r(t,k)=b_(r+1)(t,k).                           (9.1)

Dominated convergence gives continuity of b_r. This proves all coefficient
time derivatives by induction. At an arbitrary time t choose T>t, so the
compact-interval estimates provide the required local bounds.

For r,N>=0 put

  M_(T,r,N)=sup_{[0,T] x Q}|(1-Delta_y)^N F_r(t,y)|.

Compactness and the input jets make M finite. Applying periodic integration
by parts at every fixed t gives

  (1+4*pi^2*|k|^2)^N*b_r(t,k)
    = integral_Q e_{-k}(y)*(1-Delta_y)^N F_r(t,y) dy.

Since Q has volume one,

  sup_{0<=t<=T}|b_r(t,k)|
    <= M_(T,r,N)*(1+4*pi^2*|k|^2)^(-N).                (9.2)

This derives the time-uniform rapid bound from the original MagneticPath-
level regularity. The constants may depend on T,r,N. No infinite-time
uniformity or additional rapid-decay premise enters the theorem.


10. C09: ALL FOURIER JETS AND THE BOUNDARY t=0
--------------------------------------------

Define the candidate complex mixed derivatives

  Phi_(r,alpha)(t,x)
    = sum_k lambda(k)*(2*pi*i*k)^alpha*b_r(t,k)*e_k(x).

At k=0 the summand is zero. For k!=0, (9.2) gives the uniform bound

  C_(T,r,N,alpha)*(1+|k|)^(-2*N-2+|alpha|).

Choose 2*N>|alpha|+1. The decay exponent then exceeds three, so C03/M05
gives a summable majorant. Every displayed series converges absolutely and
uniformly on [0,T] x V. In particular every candidate derivative is continuous
on S. Time localization suffices; the modulus-one characters give global
uniformity in the spatial variable.

For completeness, these candidates define full multilinear Frechet jets,
not merely separate partial derivatives. Number the spacetime coordinates
0,1,2,3, with coordinate 0 temporal. For an ordered tuple
I=(i_1,...,i_q), let r(I) count its zeros and let alpha(I) count its spatial
indices. Set

  J_q(z)(v_1,...,v_q)
    = sum_{I in {0,1,2,3}^q}
        Phi_(r(I),alpha(I))(z)*product_j (v_j)_(i_j).

For q=0, the canonical identification of zero-linear maps with scalar values
gives J_0=Phi_(0,0). Finite coordinate expansion bounds the operator norm
of every Fourier summand of J_q by a finite sum of the summable majorants
already proved. Hence J_q is a continuous multilinear-map-valued function
on S for every q.

On each convex open slab (0,T) x V, (9.1) and ordinary character calculus
differentiate each summand. The derivative bounds and one-point summability
satisfy M06. Apply M06 successively to the jet series to obtain

  D J_q = curry(J_(q+1)) on O=(0,infinity) x V.         (10.1)

Here currying means that the new derivative direction is placed in the
first slot. The jets are symmetric because their coordinate coefficients
depend only on the counts r(I),alpha(I). The order of slot insertion does
not change the result.

Every J_q and J_(q+1) is continuous on S=closure(O). Consequently J_q is
continuous within O at every point of closure(O), and (10.1) has the limit
curry(J_(q+1)(z)) as y tends to z within O. The set O is convex and open.
Thus every hypothesis of the public endpoint M08 is satisfied, giving

  HasFDerivWithinAt J_q (curry(J_(q+1)(z))) S z
       for every z in S.                              (10.2)

This proves the full within Frechet derivative at t=0 as well as at positive
time. No extension theorem or negative-time values are required.

For every natural n prove simultaneously for every q that J_q is C^n on S.
For n=0 this is the established continuity. For the induction step, use the
induction hypothesis for J_(q+1), continuous linearity of currying, (10.2),
UniqueDiffOn(S), and M09. The implication involving n=omega in M09 is
vacuous for finite n; no analyticity premise is introduced. Finally M10
gives ContDiffOn R infinity for J_0 on S.

Taking real parts componentwise is continuous linear. C05 identifies the
real part of J_0 with the exact scalarL/vectorL formula on S. Smoothness
within S depends only on values on S. This proves (PL), including t=0,
for the actual total definitions regardless of their negative-time values.

The same argument, or (9.1) applied to k=0 and finite-order induction,
proves smoothness of t -> <F(t,.)>. Its lift to S has zero spatial jets.
The proof also establishes D_t L(F(t))=L(D_t F(t)) and the corresponding
identity for K by differentiating the series and the spatial curl.


11. C10: CANONICAL SMOOTHNESS H3
-------------------------------

Let MagneticPath(B). Apply (PL) to B and set

  A(t,x)=L(B(t,.))(x),
  u(t,x)=m+curl_x A(t,x).

The trajectory A is jointly smooth. Actual spatial differentiation preserves
joint smoothness on S, so u is jointly smooth. For every t>=0,

  div u=div(curl A)=0.

This argument uses neither H1, H2, nor H4.

Products, cross products, norm-square polynomials, and actual spatial
derivatives preserve joint smoothness. Hence

  G=B cross u+nu curl B,
  Q=div G,
  e=|u|^2/2

are jointly smooth on S and periodic in x. Apply scalar (PL) to Q to obtain
joint smoothness of h=LQ. The final paragraph of C09 proves smoothness of
the actual cube mean <e(t,.)>. Thus

  p(t,x)=h(t,x)-e(t,x)+<e(t,.)>

is jointly smooth on S. This is precisely the frozen canonical pressure:
linearity of the finite integral gives <e>=<|u|^2>/2. Equation (7.1) gives
<h>=0 and therefore <p>=0, fixing the scalar gauge exactly.

All three fields of CanonicalSmoothness are now proved, for every real
nu,c and every m. The parameter c does not occur in these three fields.

  MagneticPath(B) -> CanonicalSmoothness(nu,c,m,B).    (H3)

The existing canonical-field calculus then gives smooth E,rho,J. Directly,
V=-curl G is smooth periodic; (PL) makes K(V) smooth; the explicit polynomial
and spatial derivative formula for DG[B](V) therefore makes J smooth. All
spatial periodicity follows from C06 and differentiation of periodic fields.


12. C11: ELECTRIC HODGE H4 AND EXACT PRESSURE
-------------------------------------------

Let MagneticData(B), m arbitrary, and u=U_m[B]. By H2 and C07,
curl u=B and div u=0. The concrete Lamb identity is

  B cross u=(u.grad)u-grad(|u|^2/2).

For every component i, periodic integration by parts gives

  integral_Q (u.grad)u_i
    = sum_j integral_Q [partial_j(u_j*u_i)-u_i*partial_j u_j]
    = -integral_Q u_i*div u
    = 0.

The integrated total derivatives vanish by equality of opposite face values.
Likewise the mean of every periodic gradient and curl vanishes. Therefore

  <G>=<(u.grad)u-grad(|u|^2/2)+nu curl B>=0.           (12.1)

Apply (8.1) to G. Since h=L(div G), (12.1) gives

  G+grad h=K(curl G).                                 (H4)

The sign is plus. The mean-zero conclusion (12.1) is derived, including
for arbitrary m and every real nu.

Optional endpoint T07 supplies the same nonzero-mode pressure recovery via
the coefficient Leray projection

  P_0 b=b,
  P_k b=b-k*(k.b)/|k|^2                         (k!=0).

Indeed the coefficients of G+grad L(div G) are P_k Ghat(k), including its
zero mode Ghat(0). The coefficients of K(curl G) are P_k Ghat(k) for k!=0
and zero for k=0. Thus (12.1) is exactly the missing zero-mode adapter.
The full Leray projection retains constants; the frozen K curl removes them.

To identify the scalar potential itself, its coefficients are

  hhat(0)=0,
  hhat(k)=lambda(k)*2*pi*i*(k.Ghat(k))
         = i*(k.Ghat(k))/(2*pi*|k|^2)             (k!=0).

The T07 pressure has the same gradient multiplier and zero coefficient at
zero. For k!=0, at least one k_j is nonzero, so its derivative-symbol
identity fixes this scalar coefficient. Coefficient injectivity then gives
equality to the frozen h. Equivalently, a smooth periodic mean-zero harmonic
function has every Fourier coefficient zero by (3.2) and (5.1), proving
uniqueness of the mean-zero solution of -Delta h=div G.

Equality of gradients without the mean-zero condition would not identify
the exact frozen scalar potential. The direct C06/C11 route already fixes
both the coefficients and the gauge; T07 is optional.

14.1. Data correspondence.

Given Data(a), put m=<a> and B0=curl a. Smoothness and periodicity pass to
curl; div(curl a)=0 and <curl a>=0. Thus MagneticData(B0), and H1 gives
U_m[B0]=a.

data spaces. The independent mean m is retained.

14.2. Mean conservation for an NSE solution.

Integrating (NS), the convection mean vanishes by div u=0 and periodicity;
the pressure gradient and Laplacian means also vanish. Differentiation under
the cube integral, justified by joint smoothness and compactness as in C08,
gives D_t<u(t)>=0. The fundamental theorem on each compact time interval,
including the continuous value at zero, yields

  <u(t)>=<u(0)>=m.

This uses actual periodic pressure, so no nonperiodic affine pressure force
is introduced. The project already proves this exact mean-conservation step.

14.3. NSE -> constrained Maxwell.

Assume FeffermanB(nu), fix c>0, arbitrary m and MagneticData(B0). Choose
a=U_m[B0] and an NSE solution (u,p) for a. Define B=curl u. Then B is a
MagneticPath, B(0)=B0, and H1 plus mean conservation gives u=U_m[B] on S.

Let e=|u|^2/2. The curl-curl identity with div u=0 gives curl B=-Delta u.
The Lamb identity rewrites (NS) as

  u_t+G_nu,m[B]+grad(p+e)=0.

Commuting actual spatial and time derivatives, and using curl grad=0, gives

  B_t=-curl G_nu,m[B]=V_nu,m[B].

Set E=G[B], rho=div G[B], J=c^2 curl B-DG[B](V[B]). Smoothness and
periodicity follow from H3 and its canonical consequences. Gauss and Faraday
follow from these definitions and the preceding identity. C09 differentiates
the reconstruction to give u_t=K(B_t); the product rule therefore gives

  E_t=B_t cross u+B cross K(B_t)+nu curl B_t
     =DG[B](B_t)=DG[B](V[B]).

Hence E_t-c^2 curl B=-J, exactly the frozen Ampere equation. All constitutive
identities hold by construction, proving MaxwellB(nu,c).

14.4. Constrained Maxwell -> NSE.

Assume MaxwellB(nu,c), and fix Data(a). Put m=<a>, B0=curl a and obtain the
specified Maxwell solution. Its hypotheses make B a MagneticPath. Set

  u=U_m[B],
  h=L(div G[B]),
  p=h-|u|^2/2+<|u|^2>/2.

H3 gives the exact required joint smoothness and divergence constraint;
periodicity follows from the frozen Fourier definitions. H1 and B(0)=B0
give u(0)=a. Faraday and the constitutive E give B_t=-curl G[B]. C09 yields

  u_t=K(B_t)=-K(curl G[B])=-G[B]-grad h,

where H4 gives the final equality. H2 gives curl u=B, so curl B=-Delta u.
Using the Lamb identity and the spatial constancy of the pressure mean term,

  u_t+(u.grad)u+grad p
    =-G-grad h+(u.grad)u+grad h-grad(|u|^2/2)
    =-nu curl B
    =nu Delta u.

Thus (u,p) is an NSSolution for a, proving FeffermanB(nu).

14.5. Quantifiers and conclusion.

The reconstruction and analytic identities hold for all real nu,c where
they occur. Restricting to the frozen positive parameters gives exactly

  !  forall nu>0, forall c>0,
       FeffermanB(nu) <-> MaxwellB(nu,c).

No step proves the existence of an NSE or Maxwell solution without the
